# Research limitations

The project aims to estimate associations between rainfall anomalies and vegetation anomalies. These estimates are not automatically causal explanations.

## Scientific limitations to address

- NDVI can be affected by clouds, aerosols, snow, terrain, canopy saturation, and product quality flags.
- Gridded rainfall estimates have retrieval uncertainty and may be less reliable in complex terrain.
- Nepal's elevation and monsoon gradients can make a single national summary misleading.
- Autocorrelation reduces the effective information in monthly time series.
- Searching many lags and many pixels creates a multiple-testing problem.
- Missing months and unequal valid sample sizes can bias comparisons.
- Correlation does not establish that rainfall caused a vegetation response; temperature, land cover, irrigation, disturbance, and phenology may also matter.
- Spatial resampling can change apparent relationships and must be reported.

## Communication rules

Describe outputs as associations or estimated response lags unless a stronger causal design is justified. Show sample coverage and uncertainty. Do not label an area as drought-affected or unusually responsive based only on an unvalidated correlation map.

These limitations are a checklist for responsible interpretation, not evidence that each issue has already been quantified.
